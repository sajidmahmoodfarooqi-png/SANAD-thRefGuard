Help & how-to

SANAD checks that each citation is right — the right source, cited in the right place — formats to your university's handbook, and never edits a word of your prose. Everything runs on your own machine.

Getting started

  1. Open Library → Add source and paste your references (RIS or BibTeX). New to it? Choose Load a small sample set.
  2. Build a Style profile that matches your university's format.
  3. From Word or LibreOffice, insert citations and run the Integrity check.

Importing references

Use RIS or BibTeX (both export from any reference manager), or a plain numbered Typed list. SANAD stores everything locally and de-duplicates safely: re-importing the same reference collapses to one entry, but two genuinely different works are never silently merged — likely duplicates are flagged for you to review (rule R6).

Style profiles & formatting your thesis

A style profile matches your university's handbook — base citation style, "et al." threshold, ampersand, reference font, hanging indent, page margin and line spacing. It also carries your heading styles (Word's Styles gallery): a font and size for Title and Heading 1–3, and an optional switch to number headings automatically in Word's standard scheme — Heading 1 → 1, Heading 2 → 1.1, Heading 3 → 1.1.1. On the Format screen, apply a profile to your .docx: SANAD reformats body text, margins, headings and the reference list — the numbers renumber themselves as you add chapters — and never changes a word of your prose (headings included; Word renders the numbers, they're never typed in).

What the integrity check catches

After a citation looks right, SANAD checks whether it is right. Every finding explains itself and offers actions; nothing is ever changed automatically. Press Run a demo check on the Integrity screen to see it work.

RuleWhat it catchesWhat to do
R1 Year mismatchThe year you typed doesn't match the source in your library.Pick the correct year.
R2 Venue looks wrongA book resolved to a review of the book, not the book itself.Re-resolve the source.
R3 Orphaned citationA citation points to a source no longer in your library.Relink it, or remove it.
R4 Cited not listedA source in a grouped citation would drop out of the reference list.Restore the missing source.
R5 Listed not citedA reference sits in the list but nothing cites it.Cite it, or remove it.
R6 Possible duplicateTwo library entries look like the same work.Merge, or keep both.
R7 Ambiguous author/yearTwo sources share author + year with no a/b suffix.Add a/b suffixes.
R8 Cited out of contextThe sentence is about one topic but cites an unrelated source — a closer one may be in your library.Review the suggestion. SANAD suggests, you decide.

Using the Word add-in

The add-in puts SANAD inside Word — insert citations, rebuild your reference list, and run the integrity check without leaving your document. Crucially, it talks only to your local engine on 127.0.0.1, so your document and library are never sent online — that's the whole point of the one-time token step below.

  1. Load it in Word — Insert → My Add-ins → SANAD (or Upload My Add-in with the SANAD manifest, once).
  2. Connect once — here in SANAD, open Settings → Connect to Microsoft Word, click Copy, then in Word's add-in open the Connect tab, paste, and click Connect. SANAD keeps the same token across restarts, so you only do this once.
  3. Use it — Insert drops a citation at the cursor, Reference list rebuilds the bibliography, Integrity checks every citation. It only ever writes inside its own citation/reference fields — never your prose.

The "never touches your prose" guarantee

SANAD only ever writes inside its own citation and bibliography fields — it has no way to change your sentences, and this is enforced in code, not just promised. Even if SANAD is closed or uninstalled, your citations stay as ordinary, readable text; they never turn into broken links.

Your data & privacy

Everything is local. Your library is a single file on your machine; the engine listens only on 127.0.0.1 and makes no outbound connections. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.

Troubleshooting

  • Status light is red. The engine didn't start — quit and reopen SANAD. If it persists, another program may be using port 23890.
  • Import did nothing. Check the format matches what you pasted; a plain numbered list should use Typed list.
  • R8 says "semantic check: off". The optional semantic model isn't installed, so SANAD used a lighter word-overlap check — still useful, just less sensitive to paraphrases.
